# Sample draft

**Example brief**

Topic: A five-minute Friday review for solo consultants. Audience: consultants juggling several client projects. Problem: Monday starts with too many open tasks and no clear first move. Voice: direct and practical. Footage: desk, paper, pen, calendar. CTA: save the reel. Target: 30 seconds.

## Hooks

1. “Monday morning. Ten tabs open, and you still don’t know where to start.”
2. “Does Monday start with a full inbox and no clear first move?”
3. “Your laptop is open. Your task list is growing. Where do you start?”

Use hook 1. Show a staged inbox and an unfinished list with “Monday. Where do you even start?” on screen.

## Script and storyboard

| Time | Beat | Spoken line | Shot | On-screen text |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–6 sec | The problem | Monday morning. Ten tabs open, and you still don’t know where to start. | Staged inbox and unfinished list | Monday. Where do you even start? |
| 6–14 sec | Make it bigger | You reread messages, rebuild your list, and spend the morning deciding what needs attention. The work is still waiting. | Repeat tabs, add tasks, show a clock | More time deciding. Work still waiting. |
| 14–30 sec | The solution | Try a five-minute Friday review. Write what’s delivered, what’s blocked, and your next action. Put that action on Monday’s calendar. Save this for Friday. | Close tabs, write three checks, schedule one action | Choose Monday’s first move on Friday. |

## Caption

Before you sign off Friday, write down what you delivered, what’s blocked, and the first thing you’ll do Monday. Save this for your next Friday wrap-up.

## Review before filming

- Confirm that the problem and consequences fit your audience. These are fictional examples.
- Use a fictional calendar and cover client names.
- Read the script aloud and adjust the last beat to fit.
